MoonPay seeks OCC charter for custody and stablecoin services

MoonPay’s proposed national trust bank would handle stablecoin issuance, redemption and reserves, plus custody and conversions, if the OCC approves its application.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 22, 2026 · 16:38 UTC2 min read

MoonPay has applied to establish a national trust bank that could custody digital assets and manage stablecoin issuance, redemptions and reserves if the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency approves the proposal.

The application, dated Nov. 17 and received by the OCC on Nov. 18, proposes a new, non-depository bank called MoonPay National Trust Bank. The OCC’s pending applications list records the filing. MoonPay’s public charter application says the bank’s operations would be limited to trust company activities and related services.

What the proposed bank would do

The filing names three service lines. First, fiduciary custody would mean holding digital assets on behalf of clients. Second, stablecoin services would cover issuance, redemption and reserve management: the bank could issue stablecoins, process their redemption and manage the assets held in reserve. Third, it would provide conversion services as an agent, meaning it would perform conversions on a client’s behalf.

These functions describe a proposed operating model, not services the bank is already authorized to provide. The OCC has listed the application as pending. MoonPay says it is seeking no deposit insurance. The filing also marks the proposed bank as a Federal Reserve member bank.

In practical terms, the application seeks to put custody, stablecoin operations and conversions inside one federally chartered trust bank. That is like placing several related services under one roof: the proposed bank would hold client assets, handle stablecoin flows and manage reserves. The application does not establish that MoonPay has received approval or begun offering those services through the proposed bank.

Application details and next step

The proposed bank’s main office would be at 407 Lincoln Road, Suite 6-K, in Miami Beach, Florida. MoonPay Inc. is named as its corporate sponsor and would own the bank. The filing lists a five-member board, with MoonPay chief executive Ivan Soto-Wright as chair; one director remains to be determined. Michael Lempres and Caroline Butler are identified as independent directors.

The filing requests full fiduciary powers and says no separate application for those powers has been filed. The OCC’s public listing identifies the filing as pending, so the next significant step is the regulator’s review. The proposal would expand MoonPay’s role in custody and stablecoin infrastructure if approved, but the filing alone does not set a launch date or confirm which stablecoins the bank would issue.
