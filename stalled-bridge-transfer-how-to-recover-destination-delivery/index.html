Stalled Bridge Transfer: How to Recover Destination Delivery

When a bridge transfer stalls, the tokens are still safe. Learn to trace the message, trigger a retry, and get destination delivery finally completed.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 22, 2026 · 20:45 UTC4 min read

A stalled bridge transfer is recoverable: the assets are still controlled by the bridge contract, and the destination delivery can be completed by retrying the message that carries them. Bridges work by splitting a transfer into two halves. On the source chain, a contract locks or burns your tokens and emits a message that says “send these to this address on the other side.” On the destination chain, a second contract waits for that message, verifies it, then mints or releases the equivalent funds. A transfer stalls when the message is confirmed on the source side but never reaches the destination execution step.

Why does a bridge transfer get stuck?

A bridge transfer gets stuck when the source-chain message is confirmed but the destination-chain contract never executes. Each step is a potential failure point. The source transaction can be confirmed while the bridge’s relayer never picks up the message, often because the relayer network is overloaded or the attached fee was too low. If the relayer does pick it up, the destination contract can reject the attempt because the fee no longer covers the destination chain’s gas price. A nonce collision can also produce a duplicate message: the destination contract sees the same message ID and treats it as already processed. In every case, the source-side tokens stay locked or burned, and the destination account stays empty.

A portal bridge transfer can fail at any of these points; the recovery path is still the same: find the message, check its status, and trigger a redeem. The earlier explainer on portal bridge routes covers what each transfer type should deliver; this article focuses on what to do when that delivery gets stuck.

How do I check where the transfer stalled?

Take the source transaction hash into the source block explorer. The bridge contract emits an event with the full payload — destination address, token amount, and a nonce. Next, paste the same hash into the bridge’s explorer or status page. The status will show whether the source event is known, whether relayers have signed it, or whether destination execution failed. That tells you whether the problem is a missed relay or a failed redeem.

How do I recover the delivery?

Use the bridge’s own UI first. Most bridge interfaces show a pending transfer with a button that says “Redeem,” “Retry,” or “Resume.” Clicking it calls the destination contract with the signed message and forces the execution through. If the UI has no such button, you can sometimes call the destination contract’s redeem function directly by supplying the source transaction hash, the nonce, and the signed message from the bridge’s relayers. What actually happens during recovery:

  • The bridge app re-fetches the signed message from the relayer network.
  • It constructs a transaction on the destination chain with the payload and signature.
  • The destination contract verifies the signature, checks the nonce, and mints or releases the tokens.
  • Your wallet confirms the destination transaction, and the transfer is marked complete.

When should I contact support?

Contact support only after the status page shows a signed message and manual retries keep failing. If the destination chain is congested, retries can fail for hours, so wait for gas to settle before trying again. If a retry still fails with a verification error, contact the bridge’s official support and give them the source transaction hash. A missed relayer event is normal; a contract-level error is not. Never use a third-party “recovery service” that asks for your seed phrase or a signed transaction. The bridge’s own contract and UI are the only tools you need.

A bridge transfer is not a payment; it is a message delivery. Until the destination contract executes, the source-side assets are still committed.
