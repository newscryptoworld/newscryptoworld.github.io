Fairshake backs 32 House incumbents after crypto bill stalls

Fairshake will back 32 House incumbents, including six receiving $1 million each, as the crypto industry looks to the next Congress after the CLARITY Act stalled.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 9, 2026 · 07:39 UTC2 min read

Fairshake, a major crypto-aligned political group, says it will support 32 House incumbents in November’s midterm elections, including six who will each get $1 million in spending. The slate spans 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats, as the industry looks to the next Congress after a market-structure bill stalled in the Senate.

The Associated Press report on Fairshake’s House slate says all 32 candidates backed legislation that was a top industry priority. Fairshake has not disclosed how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates.

How will Fairshake support the candidates?

Fairshake’s support works through outside spending. CoinDesk reports that the super PAC buys advertising independently of the campaigns, so the campaign does not direct those ad purchases. Think of it as a separate loudspeaker: Fairshake chooses where to place its message, while each candidate’s campaign runs its own operation.

The six candidates receiving $1 million each are Democrats Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California, and Republicans French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. Hill chairs the House Financial Services Committee; Steil leads its digital-assets subcommittee. Fairshake has not said how the remaining spending will be divided across the slate.

What bill stalled, and what happens next?

The stalled measure is the CLARITY Act, which would establish a federal framework for digital-asset markets and divide oversight between regulators. The House passed it in July 2025. In September, the Senate blocked it in a procedural vote after lawmakers failed to agree on provisions including ethics guardrails for public officials’ crypto ventures, according to Roll Call’s account of the bill and Fairshake’s spending.

The vote leaves the industry’s market-structure push without a path forward in this Congress. Fairshake says it is backing candidates in both parties who support crypto; the list gives the group a way to support lawmakers who could take up the issue again in the next Congress. It does not guarantee that the bill will return or pass.

How much is still undisclosed?

Only the six $1 million commitments have public amounts. The Associated Press reported that Fairshake and its affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash on hand at the end of August, and the group would not say whether it plans further House or Senate spending before November. That leaves both the scale and placement of its remaining election budget unknown.
