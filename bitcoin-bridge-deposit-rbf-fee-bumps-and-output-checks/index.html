Bitcoin Bridge Deposit RBF Fee Bumps and Output Checks

Will an RBF fee bump invalidate a Bitcoin bridge deposit? Usually no: the replacement can count if it keeps the required bridge output, though its txid changes.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 25, 2026 · 21:05 UTC3 min read

An RBF fee bump usually does not invalidate a Bitcoin bridge deposit, provided the replacement still pays the bridge’s required amount to its deposit address. The bridge needs a confirmed transaction with the qualifying deposit output; it does not need the original transaction to confirm.

A fee bump replaces the unconfirmed transaction with a new one. The replacement has a different txid, the transaction’s identifier, because it is a new transaction with its own inputs and outputs. A stuck deposit may prompt a fee bump; TeleSwap’s unconfirmed transaction fixes cover reasons a Bitcoin payment can stay pending. A TeleSwap tracker may still show the old txid until its record is refreshed or updated.

The replacement counts if it keeps the required bridge output

A Bitcoin transaction’s outputs specify how much goes to each destination script. One output might pay the bridge’s deposit address, while another returns change to the wallet. The miner fee is the difference between the value of the inputs and the value of all outputs.

When a wallet bumps a fee using RBF, it creates a replacement transaction with a higher fee; it does not attach a fee to the old transaction. The wallet may cover the increase by reducing the change output. The bridge output can remain unchanged, so the replacement can still serve as the deposit once confirmed.

Consider an illustrative deposit where the bridge requires at least 0.01 BTC. The unconfirmed transaction sends that amount to the bridge and returns the remaining value as change. To speed it up, the wallet creates an RBF replacement with a higher miner fee and less change, while keeping the 0.01 BTC bridge output intact. If that replacement confirms, the bridge can recognize the deposit from its output, subject to the bridge’s own processing and confirmation requirements.

The outcome changes if the replacement removes the bridge output, sends it to a different address, or reduces its amount below the bridge’s requirement. In that case, the confirmed transaction may not qualify as the intended deposit. A fee bump alone does not tell you which outputs the wallet kept: inspect the replacement transaction itself.

Use the replacement txid to verify the deposit

After an RBF replacement, look up the new txid in your wallet or a Bitcoin transaction explorer. Confirm that the transaction is confirmed, then inspect its outputs for the expected bridge address and amount. Do not treat the original txid’s status as the final word: it may show as replaced or remain unconfirmed even though the replacement confirmed.

For a TeleSwap deposit, the tracker’s displayed txid can lag behind the wallet’s replacement. Compare the tracker entry with the confirmed transaction, and refresh or update the record if the interface offers that option. If the replacement has the correct output but the tracker still shows the original, the display may need updating; verify the transaction and follow the bridge’s support process if the deposit remains unrecognized.

Related

Can an RBF replacement change the amount sent?

Yes. The replacement can change any output, including the amount sent to the bridge. Wallets often reduce change to pay a higher fee, but inspect the bridge output to make sure its destination and amount still meet the deposit requirement.

Why does a bridge still show my old transaction?

A bridge may still show the old transaction because its tracker has not yet reflected the replacement’s new txid. Compare the tracker with the confirmed replacement and its outputs. The practical tip is to save the new txid and verify its bridge-address output; this avoids mistaking a stale tracker entry for a failed deposit.
