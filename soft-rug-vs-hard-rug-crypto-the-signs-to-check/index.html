Soft rug vs hard rug crypto: the signs to check

Soft rug vs hard rug crypto comes down to speed and control: hard rugs drain or trap funds abruptly; soft rugs unfold through team sales or abandonment.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 20, 2026 · 01:28 UTC3 min read

Token buyers comparing a soft rug with a hard rug need to know whether funds are at risk from a sudden on-chain move or a slower team exit. A hard rug abruptly drains or traps funds through on-chain control; a soft rug unfolds as the team gradually sells or abandons the project. The methods leave different traces, so the checks differ.

  • Hard rug: watch liquidity access, sell restrictions and contract controls.
  • Soft rug: track team wallets, token allocations, unlock dates and project activity.
  • A purchase-time rugcheck can flag hard-rug setups; soft rugs call for repeat wallet checks.

How does a hard rug drain or trap funds?

A hard rug uses a sudden action to remove the assets buyers rely on or to stop them selling. In a token’s Liquidity pool, holders sell into the pool’s paired assets. When the creator withdraws unlocked liquidity, those assets leave the pool and the price can collapse within one block.

Picture a buyer opening a token chart and seeing a steep drop in a single block. The creator has withdrawn unlocked LP from the Liquidity pool, leaving holders with far fewer assets to sell into. A rugcheck can help surface this kind of setup at purchase by reviewing token risks such as liquidity and control. The linked rugcheck topic adds useful background on what that review checks.

A hard rug can also trap holders by blocking sells through contract controls. An active Mint authority is another warning sign: it allows new supply to be created, which can then be dumped into the market. The Token creator wallet and contract permissions help show who may be able to make those moves.

How does a soft rug unfold, and what should you monitor?

A soft rug develops over time as the team sells tokens steadily or stops supporting the project. Its traces accumulate across team wallet activity and project updates instead of appearing as one abrupt liquidity event.

Say the team’s wallets sell a portion of their holdings each week while project updates stop over several weeks. The price may weaken gradually as those sales add supply. Holder concentration matters here: when a small number of wallets control large balances, their sales can have a greater effect on available supply and the market.

At purchase, note the team’s token allocations and any published unlock dates. Then recheck those wallets on a schedule, looking for transfers to trading venues or repeated sales. Compare the activity with the project’s updates: a quiet project alongside ongoing team sales is a different pattern from one isolated wallet movement.

Use the two checks at different times. At purchase, review the Liquidity pool, whether liquidity is unlocked, contract permissions such as Mint authority, and the Token creator wallet. These checks can flag a hard-rug setup before you buy. For a soft rug, record team allocations and unlock dates at purchase, then monitor the relevant wallets over the following weeks. A one-time rugcheck is a snapshot; ongoing wallet monitoring can reveal a gradual exit that was not visible at the start.
