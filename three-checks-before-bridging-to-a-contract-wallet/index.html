Three checks before bridging to a contract wallet

A bridge can deliver tokens to an address without making its wallet ready to use them. Check the route, destination wallet and control rules first.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 16, 2026 · 22:40 UTC4 min read

Before bridging, check that the route supports your contract wallet, that the destination address is correct on the receiving network, and that you can use the funds after they arrive. A bridge moves assets between networks through a route that may lock or burn tokens on the source chain, then release or mint them on the destination chain. The wallet address is the delivery address; it does not tell you whether the wallet is deployed there or whether it can act on the funds.

Does the bridge route support a contract wallet?

Check the route’s recipient rules before signing. A contract wallet is controlled by code, such as owner approvals or a signature threshold, while a standard wallet address is controlled directly by a private key. A bridge may accept a destination address without checking what kind of account it is. Some routes deliver tokens only; others may support a destination action, such as calling a contract after delivery. Those are different services.

Read the route summary and transaction details for the destination chain, token and recipient address. Confirm whether the route supports contract-wallet recipients and whether it requires a separate wallet signature or transaction to claim the funds. The fuller manta bridge guide compares route mechanics for treasury and payout flows. The key point here is to match the route to the action you need: receiving a token is not the same as executing a transaction from your wallet.

Is the wallet ready at the destination address?

Confirm the address on the receiving network, then check whether the wallet contract is deployed there. A contract wallet may use the same address across networks, but its code and setup are network-specific. Some wallets deploy only when first used. If yours is not deployed on the destination chain, the address may still receive a token, but you may need a deployment step before the wallet can manage it.

Check the wallet’s owner or signer setup, any approval threshold, and whether it supports the token you plan to bridge. A token transfer can succeed even when the wallet’s interface does not display the asset or its controls are not configured as expected. Use the wallet’s own address and network details, not an address copied from a different account or chain.

Can you move or use the funds after delivery?

Check the wallet’s execution rules and the destination chain’s transaction requirements. To spend a token, the wallet must authorize a transaction that calls the token contract. Depending on the wallet and route, that may require owner signatures, a relayer, or a separate gas payment. A bridge’s delivery step does not automatically provide those things.

Before sending the full amount, confirm that you can meet the wallet’s signing requirements and pay any needed destination fees. If the wallet has a threshold, make sure enough owners can approve a transaction. If a relayer or paymaster is involved, verify that the wallet and action are eligible under its rules. Treat a displayed balance as evidence of receipt, not proof that spending is ready.

  • Route: Does it accept this contract-wallet recipient and deliver the intended token to the right chain?
  • Wallet: Is the wallet deployed or deployable at that address, with its owners and controls set up?
  • Use: Can you authorize the next transaction and meet its fee requirements?

For most users, the better choice is a route that clearly supports the destination network and recipient, with wallet setup confirmed before the transfer. A small test transfer can reveal address, deployment or display problems while less value is at stake. Once it arrives, verify the token on the destination chain and check that the wallet can authorize a spend. That completes the path from bridge delivery to usable funds.
