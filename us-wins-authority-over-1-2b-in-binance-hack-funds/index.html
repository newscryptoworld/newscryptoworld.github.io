US wins authority over $1.2B in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion from Binance’s $6 billion hack, while tracing and freezes continue.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 16, 2026 · 16:20 UTC2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as investigators and private companies continue tracing the funds. The department’s Nov. 12 announcement says more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets has been frozen so far.

How did authorities move against the stolen crypto?

According to the Justice Department, members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. After the theft, U.S. agencies worked with virtual asset service providers, the companies that handle crypto transactions, to trace and freeze assets before they moved beyond law enforcement’s reach.

A freeze puts a hold on funds so they cannot be transferred through a participating service. The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion within the larger frozen total. It allows the government to seize or restrain those assets; the announcement does not say that all $1.2 billion has been transferred into government custody.

The FBI also urged service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the Binance funds. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors have continued moving assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. A bridge moves tokens between blockchains; a mixer pools transactions to obscure their trail. Each route creates another point where investigators and providers may try to identify or stop a transfer.

Who is affected by the freeze?

The immediate target is the stolen crypto and the addresses or services handling it. Binance reported the theft, while U.S. officials attribute it to TraderTraitor. The freeze total reflects combined government and private-sector action, rather than a recovery figure for Binance customers.

The Associated Press report notes that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department’s announcement does not say the funds have been returned or identify who will ultimately receive them.

What does the court authorization change?

It gives the government legal authority to pursue a defined portion of the funds already frozen. That is a step beyond tracing or asking providers to block transfers, but it does not settle ownership or guarantee recovery. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are still underway.
