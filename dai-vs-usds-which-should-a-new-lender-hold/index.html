DAI vs USDS: Which Should a New Lender Hold?

DAI vs USDS for lending comes down to each venue’s rate, rewards and integrations: they convert 1:1, but each token earns from its own reserve.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 19, 2026 · 16:18 UTC4 min read

For a new lender, DAI vs USDS comes down to which token earns more in the venue you plan to use, after rewards and limits; the tokens convert 1:1. SparkLend makes the distinction concrete: its separate DAI and USDS reserves have their own supply rates and caps. Each token also has a different savings route and set of integrations, so compare the terms where you intend to deposit.

What is the difference between DAI and USDS for lending?

DAI is Sky’s legacy stablecoin, and USDS is its successor, launched in 2024 as part of the move from MakerDAO to Sky. The official converter swaps DAI and USDS at a fixed 1:1 rate in either direction. So 10,000 DAI converts to exactly 10,000 USDS through that route.

That conversion sets the exchange amount; it does not set your lending return. Think of the tokens as two tickets to separate lending pools: each pool has its own cash, borrowers, supply rate and limits. A 1:1 swap means you can change which pool you enter without changing the token count, but it does not make the pools pay the same yield.

On SparkLend, supply rates respond to the terms of each reserve. Borrow demand and utilization affect the rate, while governance-set parameters also shape DAI and USDS rates. Rewards can add to a displayed return, but they may change. Supply caps can limit how much a reserve accepts, and available liquidity can affect withdrawals.

For instance, suppose you have 10,000 DAI. Before depositing, compare the DAI and USDS supply rates, any active rewards, the remaining supply cap and the amount available to withdraw. If USDS has the better overall terms and room for your deposit, convert the DAI 1:1 and supply USDS. If DAI’s terms suit you better, keep the DAI and use that reserve.

The trade-off is practical: USDS can open Sky Token Rewards and newer Sky integrations, while DAI has a wider legacy footprint across older applications. Rewards may improve USDS’s return, but checking them and the reserve limits adds a step. If you need an integration that only supports DAI, that access can matter more than a higher quoted USDS rate. Choose based on the venue you’ll actually use, not the token name alone.

Can I convert USDS back to DAI?

Yes. The official converter swaps USDS back to DAI 1:1, just as it swaps DAI to USDS. Check the converter and network before you transact, and account for any network transaction costs.

What is sUSDS?

sUSDS is the token you receive when you put USDS into Sky’s savings module. It tracks a position earning the Sky Savings Rate, which can change over time. The comparable DAI route issues sDAI, which represents a position earning the DAI Savings Rate. These savings tokens are distinct from supplying DAI or USDS to a lending reserve: the rate source and product mechanics differ.

What should I check before choosing a token?

  • Compare the live supply rate and rewards for each reserve.
  • Check the supply cap and liquidity for the amount you plan to deposit.
  • Confirm the token and network work with your wallet and intended strategy.

Then supply the token whose current terms fit your plan. That comparison tells you what return and access you’re choosing, while the 1:1 converter gives you a route to switch tokens if the terms change.

What should a DAI or USDS holder know next?

What backs DAI and USDS?

Both are Sky stablecoins backed by collateral managed through the Sky Protocol. The collateral mix and system parameters can change, so consult Sky’s current onchain information when assessing backing risk.

Does USDS have a freeze function?

The current Ethereum USDS token contract has no freeze function. Its contract is upgradeable, however, and Sky governance could change it; token implementations can also differ by network.

Is the DAI Savings Rate still paid?

sDAI remains the savings token for the DAI Savings Rate. The rate can change, so check the current rate and whether the savings route is available before depositing.

Can I use USDS on Layer 2 networks?

USDS is available on some Layer 2 networks, but support depends on the chain and whether its token is native or bridged. Verify the network and contract address before sending funds.
