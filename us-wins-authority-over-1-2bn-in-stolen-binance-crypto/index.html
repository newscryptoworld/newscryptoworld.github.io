US wins authority over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department secured authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in Binance theft proceeds, part of $2.7 billion frozen so far.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 17, 2026 · 04:28 UTC3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as investigators and private companies work to stop the funds moving through crypto services. The department said Thursday that more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets has been frozen so far, according to its announcement of the Binance asset action.

How did investigators stop the stolen crypto?

On or about Oct. 29, North Korean military members identified by the FBI as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance, the Justice Department said. Once cryptocurrency leaves an exchange, it can move between digital wallets and across services. Investigators therefore worked with virtual asset service providers to identify and interdict funds linked to the theft.

A freeze blocks assets from being transferred through a service that controls or can restrict them. It does not, by itself, decide who legally owns the funds or permanently transfer them to the government. The department says it has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the more than $2.7 billion frozen through joint government and private-sector efforts. Those are related but distinct steps.

The FBI has also urged virtual asset providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the Binance funds. The Justice Department says the actors continue to move assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. A bridge routes assets between blockchain networks; a mixer can obscure transaction links. Each service can create another point where investigators and providers may try to identify or stop a transfer.

Who is linked to the theft, and what happens to the funds?

The FBI attributes the Binance theft to TraderTraitor, which the Justice Department describes as members of the North Korean military. The government’s claim is an attribution by U.S. authorities. The department’s announcement says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts are continuing.

Seizure or restraint authority gives the government a legal route to keep assets under control while it pursues the case. It does not mean the funds have already been returned to Binance or to individual customers. In a typical forfeiture process, the government files a claim and a judge decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets, the Associated Press reported in its coverage of the FBI announcement.

What changes for Binance customers?

The freeze reduces the amount of stolen crypto that can be moved through services cooperating with authorities. But the $1.2 billion figure refers to assets covered by court-authorized seizure or restraint, while the larger $2.7 billion figure refers to assets frozen by combined public and private efforts. The Justice Department says work to trace and recover the remaining stolen assets is ongoing. Whether and how any recovered funds reach Binance or affected customers depends on the later legal process.
