Fed and OCC ask banks about systems after Binance hack

After Binance reported a $6.3 billion hot-wallet loss, Fed and OCC supervisors asked banks about unusual activity and outages, prompting security checks.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 8, 2026 · 13:33 UTC3 min read

US bank supervisors contacted banks after Binance’s reported $6.3 billion hack, asking about unusual activity and service disruptions as they checked whether the breach posed risks to traditional finance. The Reuters report said Federal Reserve and Office of the Comptroller of the Currency supervisors began reaching out on Monday morning, November 2, according to people familiar with the matter.

The sequence was a check-in, followed by banks’ own system reviews. Supervisors asked banks whether they had seen unusual activity or disruptions. Separately, banks studied the attack and ran diagnostics on their systems to see whether they could defend against similar tactics. Reuters reported that one contacted bank had experienced no service disruption. The reported outreach shows regulators checking for possible spillover; it does not establish that Binance’s breach reached a bank.

What did Binance say was compromised?

Binance said unauthorized withdrawals from its hot wallets caused the loss. A hot wallet is the part of an exchange’s crypto storage used for online transactions. In its October 30 security update, Binance put the loss at about $6.3 billion in token assets and said its investigation had found additional breach points beyond an initially suspected compromised laptop. The company said it had found no evidence that wallet private keys were leaked. Its investigation was ongoing.

Binance also said it would cover the stolen funds through its Secure Asset Fund for Users and corporate treasury. It reported that about $3 billion had been frozen on-chain, and that it had temporarily suspended withdrawals while it checked affected systems. Those were Binance’s stated response steps; they do not show whether any bank systems were exposed.

Why did regulators contact banks?

Reuters described supervisors as alert to the risk that a crypto-market hack could affect traditional finance as links between the two sectors have tightened. The reported bank checks followed that concern: ask about unusual activity or service interruption, then assess defenses and possible vulnerabilities. Banks were also reviewing the attack internally. At one large US bank, a cybersecurity team contacted Binance within hours and planned an internal report that could lead to a review of the bank’s systems and cybersecurity updates, Reuters reported.

The report does not identify the banks contacted or describe a confirmed route from Binance’s compromised wallets into bank systems. Binance said it was unaware of conversations between bankers and regulators or of any impact on US banks. The Fed and OCC declined to comment. So far, the confirmed development is supervisory outreach and bank-side diagnostics after the hack, with no disruption reported by the bank source cited by Reuters.
