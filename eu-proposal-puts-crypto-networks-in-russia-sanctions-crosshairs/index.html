EU proposal puts crypto networks in Russia sanctions crosshairs

The Commission proposed targeting crypto and payment networks tied to Russia, alongside restrictions on 60 banks, putting new EU sanctions before member states.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 25, 2026 · 00:31 UTC3 min read

The European Commission has proposed targeting crypto and payment networks it says help Russia evade sanctions, as part of its 22nd sanctions package announced on 23 November. The Associated Press report on the proposal says the measures need approval from all 27 EU member states before they can take effect.

How the proposal would reach crypto networks

The Commission’s announcement names crypto and payment networks as targets, including networks in third countries that help Russia evade existing restrictions. It does not identify specific platforms or set out the detailed rules for applying those measures. The Commission says it has launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support the work and align efforts with international partners.

The distinction matters: the proposal sets a direction for enforcement, but the announcement does not describe a new crypto transaction ban in operational detail. For comparison, it is more like naming the routes authorities intend to close than publishing a list of every platform affected. The task force is part of the Commission’s plan to pursue those routes; the proposal still needs approval from member states.

What the bank measures would change

For banks, the Commission has specified a transaction ban on about 60 Russian banks and says all 60 would be removed from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use to communicate about international payments. The Commission president’s statement also says the package would focus on third countries that help Russia evade sanctions.

A transaction ban restricts dealings with the named banks; removal from SWIFT cuts their access to that messaging network. These are separate measures, aimed at the banking channel. The crypto proposal broadens the focus to digital-asset and payment networks, but the Commission has not yet supplied the specific platform names or compliance steps in its announcement.

When the measures could take effect

The package remains a proposal. According to AP, every EU member state must endorse it, a process that can take weeks, and the measures may change before adoption. Until then, the announcement does not itself impose the proposed restrictions on crypto platforms or the 60 banks.

The crypto stakes therefore turn on what member states approve and how the Commission’s broad target is translated into rules. The confirmed change is that Brussels has placed crypto and payment networks, including those in third countries accused of helping Russia evade sanctions, inside its next proposed package. The task force is intended to support that effort, while the final scope remains unsettled.
