Bitcoin ETFs lose $582 million as November outflows hit $3.47B

US spot bitcoin ETFs lost $582.2 million on Nov. 19, pushing November net outflows to $3.47 billion and showing how redemptions reshape fund holdings.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 21, 2026 · 06:17 UTC3 min read

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs recorded $582.2 million in net outflows on Thursday, Nov. 19, their third straight day of withdrawals, according to The Block’s report on the daily ETF flows. The redemptions brought November’s net outflow total to about $3.47 billion through that session.

These funds hold bitcoin while their shares trade on stock exchanges. Investors buy and sell shares through brokers. When demand changes enough, authorized participants—large financial firms that can transact directly with a fund—create or redeem blocks of shares. In a redemption, shares go back to the fund, which settles by transferring bitcoin or, depending on the fund’s process, selling bitcoin for cash. The reported net flow is the difference between money entering and leaving the funds.

Which funds accounted for the Nov. 19 outflow?

BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust, ticker IBIT, was the largest source of withdrawals. It recorded $541.1 million in outflows, followed by Fidelity’s FBTC at $45.9 million, Ark 21Shares’ ARKB at $31.3 million and Grayscale’s GBTC at $13.9 million, according to The Block.

Morgan Stanley’s MSBT moved in the other direction, taking in $49.6 million. WisdomTree’s BTCW also had a small inflow. The net total combines withdrawals and deposits across the funds, so it is smaller than the sum of the largest funds’ reported outflows.

How does a redemption affect a fund’s bitcoin?

When an authorized participant redeems shares, the fund’s share count and assets fall. If redemption is settled in cash, the fund may sell bitcoin to provide that cash. If it is settled in kind, bitcoin is transferred instead. The ETF flow number therefore measures net movement into and out of the funds; it does not mean every dollar of net outflow became a bitcoin sale on an exchange.

Think of the ETF as a container with exchange-traded shares representing claims on its bitcoin holdings. A redemption returns some claims and requires the fund to settle them. The exact settlement route depends on the fund. The November figure captures the combined flow across products, not the activity of every bitcoin holder.

How large is November’s withdrawal so far?

Through Nov. 19, U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had recorded only two positive-flow sessions during November, totaling $82.5 million on Nov. 4 and Nov. 16. The month-to-date net outflow was about $3.47 billion, according to The Block. The funds had attracted about $53.5 billion in cumulative net inflows since launching in January 2024 and held $76.6 billion in assets as of Nov. 19.

Farside Investors’ fund-by-fund bitcoin ETF flow table lists the Nov. 19 total as $582.3 million, a slight difference from The Block’s $582.2 million figure. Both show the same pattern: a sharp net withdrawal led by IBIT, with MSBT among the funds receiving deposits. One day’s flow records a change in these funds’ holdings; by itself, it does not establish what caused bitcoin’s price movement.
