How to Choose Slippage for Base Token Swaps

Set Base swap slippage to the output shortfall you accept; pool depth, trade size and price movement determine whether the transaction clears its limit.

Crypto Record EditorialSep 29, 20262 min read

Choose slippage for a Base token swap by setting the largest drop in output you are willing to accept before the transaction fails. The swap interface estimates how much of the output token your input should buy, then turns your tolerance into a minimum-output limit. The transaction only completes if the route delivers at least that amount.

What does slippage tolerance change?

Slippage tolerance sets the boundary for execution, not the price the pool offers. If a quote estimates 1,000 output tokens and tolerance is 1%, the transaction’s minimum output is 990 tokens. If the route returns less, the swap reverts instead of completing below your limit.

The quote can change between the time you submit and the time the transaction executes. Pool trades, a changing route, or other activity can move the price during that gap. Slippage tolerance gives the transaction room for that movement; it does not guarantee a better quote or protect against every bad price.

How do trade size and liquidity affect the setting?

A swap draws tokens from one or more liquidity pools. In a shallow pool, a larger trade consumes more of the available side and moves the pool price further. That movement is price impact: it is already reflected in the quoted output. Slippage tolerance covers the additional change between the quote and execution.

Think of a pool as a shallow bowl of water. A small scoop barely changes the level; a large scoop does. The comparison helps explain why the same token pair can need different tolerances for different trade sizes. A route split across pools may reduce the impact on any one pool, but the quoted output is what matters for your transaction.

Before signing, compare the expected output with the minimum output shown by the interface. Consider these checks:

  • For a deep, stable pair and a modest trade, begin with a tight tolerance.
  • For a thin pool or a larger order, check whether the quote already shows substantial price impact; consider reducing the order or reviewing the route before widening tolerance.
  • If a swap fails, refresh the quote and check whether the price or route changed before raising the limit.
  • For a token with transfer fees or unusual contract rules, confirm how the interface handles those mechanics; they can affect the amount received.

When should you raise or lower slippage?

Raise tolerance only when you accept the wider range of possible output and a refreshed quote still supports the trade. A wider limit can help a transaction clear during price movement, but it also allows execution at a worse price. In some transaction-ordering conditions, it can leave more room for another trade to move the price against yours.

Lower tolerance when the minimum output falls below what you would accept. If the transaction then fails, the limit has done its job: it prevented execution below your floor, though a failed transaction may still incur network fees. Recheck the quote rather than repeatedly increasing the setting. For most swaps, a tight limit you understand is a better starting point than a broad one chosen just to force a transaction through.
