SyncSwap: How a first swap and liquidity deposit work

SyncSwap swaps tokens through on-chain pools and lets users deposit assets to earn a share of fees, with price and liquidity risks to weigh first.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 21, 2026 · 14:25 UTC5 min read

SyncSwap lets a wallet trade tokens or deposit them into a pool, and the first step is making sure the wallet holds assets on the network selected in the app. A pool is a smart contract that holds two tokens; traders exchange one for the other, while liquidity providers supply the tokens available for those trades. The interface at syncswap prepares transactions, but the wallet still asks the user to approve and sign them.

Start by connecting a wallet and checking its network against the one selected in SyncSwap. The wallet needs the token being spent and enough of that network’s native token to pay transaction fees. A token shown on a different network is not automatically usable here. If the wallet lacks funds on the selected network, obtain them through a route you trust before continuing.

How do you make a first syncswap trade?

A swap draws tokens from a pool and returns the other token, minus the pool’s fee and any network fee. In a classic automated market maker pool, the contract adjusts the price as the relative amounts of its two tokens change. A larger trade against a shallow pool can move the price more than a smaller trade. The quoted amount is therefore an estimate based on the pool state, not a fixed promise.

Select the token to spend and the token to receive, then enter an amount. Check the estimated output, price impact and minimum received before proceeding. The minimum is a slippage limit: if the pool changes too much before the transaction executes, the trade should fail instead of completing below that threshold. A very loose limit can permit a worse result; an overly tight one can cause a valid trade to fail.

If this is the wallet’s first use of that token in the app, the wallet may request a separate approval transaction. Approval lets the contract spend up to an authorized amount of that token. Review the token and spender shown in the wallet, sign the approval if correct, then review and sign the swap. The transaction executes on the selected network, and the wallet or transaction explorer can show whether it completed.

How does providing liquidity on SyncSwap work?

Liquidity provision sends tokens to a pool so traders can swap against them. In return, the provider receives a claim on part of the pool, represented according to the pool type. SyncSwap’s documented pool types include Classic, Stable and Aqua: Classic pools serve general token pairs, Stable pools use a curve designed for similarly priced assets, and Aqua pools use a concentrated-liquidity model with an auto-rebalancing mechanism.

To deposit, open the liquidity area, choose a pool and inspect its token pair and pool type. Enter the amount for one token; the interface may calculate the matching amount of the other based on the pool’s current ratio. Review both amounts and the position details before submitting. Depending on the token and wallet’s prior approvals, you may need to approve one or both assets, then sign the deposit transaction.

A useful analogy is a shared till: deposits stock the till, and traders pay to exchange what is inside. The analogy stops there, because token prices can move independently. As the pool rebalances to serve trades, a provider’s share can end up worth less than simply holding the same tokens outside the pool. Fees may offset some of that difference, but they are not guaranteed to do so.

  • Check that the pair and network are the ones you intend to use.
  • For a first position, choose a pool whose token behavior you understand.
  • Compare the deposit amounts and any displayed fee or position terms before signing.
  • Keep enough native network token for approvals, deposits and later withdrawals.

What should a first-time liquidity provider check?

Pool type affects how liquidity is used. A Stable pool is built for assets expected to stay near one another in price; it does not remove the risk that they diverge. Aqua concentrates liquidity and rebalances automatically, but its mechanics and exposure differ from simply holding two tokens. Read the position summary in the app rather than assuming every pool has the same behavior.

Before depositing, decide what loss you could tolerate if either token falls or the pair moves apart. Start with an amount you can afford to leave exposed, and note where the app shows your position so you can later remove liquidity. Withdrawal returns the position’s current underlying assets, not necessarily the original token quantities; the value and mix may have changed while the assets were in the pool.

The practical sequence is simple: verify the network and assets, compare the transaction preview with your intent, and sign only after checking the wallet prompt. A swap exchanges tokens immediately at the pool’s current terms. A liquidity deposit takes on the additional job of holding a changing pool position in exchange for a possible share of trading fees.
