Full range vs concentrated liquidity: a beginner’s first setup

Full range vs concentrated liquidity: beginners should start full-range for simpler upkeep; concentrated can earn more per dollar but needs range checks and rebalancing.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 25, 2026 · 21:02 UTC4 min read

A beginner should start with full-range liquidity because it works at every price and needs no range management. Concentrated liquidity can earn more fees per dollar, but only while the market price stays inside the range you choose.

How do fees, price range and risk change your results?

Full-range liquidity backs trades at every price, from zero to infinity. In a V2-style pool, the contract uses the constant-product formula x*y=k to adjust the token balances as traders swap. Your deposit stays available to the pool across the full price curve.

Concentrated liquidity asks you to set a minimum and maximum price. Your deposit backs trades only inside that band. Uniswap V3 introduced this model, and each position is represented by an ERC-721 NFT.

The price range determines when your capital earns fees. A narrower range puts more of your deposit to work around the current price, which can earn more fees per dollar while the price stays inside it. But a narrow band leaves less room for price movement before the position goes inactive.

When the price moves outside your range, the position stops earning fees. It also becomes entirely one token: the token on the side the price moved past. You can wait for the price to return, or pay gas to move the range. Until then, that deposit earns no trading fees.

Impermanent loss is another factor. Both models expose you to it when the token prices move relative to each other. A narrow concentrated range can raise that exposure while the position remains in range, so higher fee potential comes with more sensitivity to price movement.

  • Fee earnings per dollar: Concentrated liquidity can earn more inside its range; full-range spreads your deposit across all prices.
  • Time spent monitoring: Full-range is set-and-check; concentrated positions need regular price and range checks.
  • Gas for adjustments: Full-range needs no range changes; moving a concentrated range costs gas, which can be real money on Ethereum.
  • Impermanent-loss sensitivity: Concentrated positions can react more sharply to price movement within a narrow band.
  • Pair volatility: A volatile pair is more likely to leave a tight range; a stable pair may be easier to keep in range.

On shiba swap, pools use the V2-style constant-product model, so every deposit is full-range. That gives a beginner the simpler setup by default; the full-range versus concentrated choice comes up on V3-style DEXs such as Uniswap, SushiSwap or PancakeSwap when they offer both models.

Which setup suits a beginner’s time and experience?

Full-range suits a beginner who wants a position that keeps earning across price changes without choosing a band. It still carries impermanent-loss risk, and pool fees vary with trading activity, but you do not need to rebalance a range when the market moves.

Concentrated liquidity suits someone who understands the pair, can choose a price range and will check the position often. A stablecoin pair may make a narrow range easier to manage while prices remain close, but the position can still leave its range and stop earning fees.

Use this rule: choose full-range if you will not check the position weekly or the pair is volatile. Consider concentrated liquidity if the pair is stable and you are prepared to monitor and rebalance it. Before depositing, confirm which model the DEX pool uses; on shiba swap, the answer is full-range.
