Three Signals That Show a Bridge Deposit Is Complete

A bridge deposit is complete only when the destination transaction succeeds and settles; source confirmation and a pending relay are earlier stages, not proof of delivery.

Crypto Record EditorialSep 30, 20263 min read

A bridge deposit is complete when the destination chain records a successful transaction and the received asset is available there. The process usually starts with a source-chain transaction that locks or burns tokens. A bridge’s contract or validators then verify that event, and a relayer or other bridge component carries the message to the destination. The destination contract releases or creates the corresponding tokens. Each step has its own status, so a source-chain confirmation alone does not prove delivery.

What happens after a bridge deposit?

The bridge must carry proof of the source transaction to the destination before the destination contract can act. Think of it like sending a parcel: a receipt from the sender shows dispatch, while delivery requires a separate confirmation at the destination. The analogy ends there; on a bridge, contracts and verification rules determine when the next step can proceed.

Some bridges use validators or relayers to pass messages; others use different verification designs. The route also matters: a bridge may lock tokens on one chain and release tokens held on another, or burn tokens on the source and mint them on the destination. A fuller walkthrough of moving tokens through Polygon Bridge shows how these stages fit together for one route.

Which three signals show the deposit is done?

Look for evidence from the destination chain, not just a completed source transaction. These three signals mark progress from dispatch to usable funds:

  • The source transaction succeeded. Its receipt should show success on the source chain, with the expected token and amount. This proves the deposit reached the bridge’s source-side contract; it does not prove that the destination step has run.
  • The bridge message was processed. The bridge’s status or message record should move beyond pending, failed, or waiting for verification. This indicates the route accepted the source event and advanced it for delivery. A relayer submitting a transaction is useful evidence, but that transaction can still fail.
  • The destination transaction succeeded. Find the destination-chain transaction and confirm its status is successful. Check that the receiving address, token and amount match the deposit. The asset should appear in that address’s balance and be available for the action you intend to take.

These signals are related but not interchangeable. The first establishes that the source event happened. The second shows that the bridge has progressed the message. The third establishes that the destination contract completed the transfer. A status label such as “complete” is less conclusive than a successful destination transaction and a matching balance.

What if the bridge says complete but the tokens are missing?

Check the destination transaction before repeating the deposit. Confirm that you are viewing the destination chain and the address used for the transfer, then verify the token contract and amount in the transaction details. A wallet may not display a token automatically even when the balance exists on-chain.

If there is no successful destination transaction, the deposit may still be waiting for verification or relay, or the destination attempt may have failed. Follow the route’s own status and support process, and avoid sending a second deposit just because the first has not appeared yet. For most readers, the safest completion check is simple: match the source receipt to the bridge message, then match that message to a successful destination transaction and balance.
