Ethereum-to-Sui waits on finality; the return needs an ETH claim

Suibridge Ethereum to Sui vs Sui to Ethereum: compare steps, waits and gas. Outbound waits for Ethereum finality; return needs an Ethereum claim.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 26, 2026 · 13:46 UTC4 min read

Suibridge completes Ethereum-to-Sui transfers after Ethereum finality, while Sui-to-Ethereum transfers need a separate Ethereum claim. To move a supported asset into Sui, Suibridge provides the bridge for that direction; the main difference is that Ethereum finality sets the outbound wait, while the return depends on a claim you sign and pay for in ETH. The bridge moves assets through lock-and-mint on the way to Sui, and burn-and-release on the way back.

How does Suibridge differ by direction?

Ethereum to Sui has more waiting before the destination asset is minted; Sui to Ethereum has a quick Sui step followed by an Ethereum transaction. The stages differ because each chain finalizes transactions in its own way.

  • Ethereum to Sui — approve, deposit, then wait. For an ERC-20, approve the transfer and deposit the asset on Ethereum. After Ethereum finality, the bridge mints the corresponding asset on Sui. That mint happens without another action from you.
  • Sui to Ethereum — burn, wait for signatures, then claim. Burn the asset on Sui, then wait for Sui validators to sign off. Once the transfer is ready, sign a claim transaction on Ethereum to release the asset. The claim uses ETH for Ethereum gas.
  • Gas — pay on the chains where your transactions happen. The outbound route uses Ethereum gas for approval and deposit. The return uses cheap Sui gas for the burn, then Ethereum gas for the claim. Have ETH in the Ethereum wallet that will submit that claim.
  • Rate limits — check the direction you need. Limits apply by route and direction. One route may be held while another continues, so activity in one direction does not tell you whether the other is available.

Consider an illustrative transfer of a supported asset. Before the outbound transfer, it is on Ethereum; after the deposit and mint, its bridged representation is on Sui. On the return, the Sui representation is burned, then the Ethereum-side asset is released when you claim. The asset’s location and representation change at each stage; the return also leaves the transfer awaiting your Ethereum transaction.

Choose Ethereum to Sui when you want the asset on Sui and can wait about 13 minutes for Ethereum finality; after that, minting needs no further action. Choose Sui to Ethereum when you want the asset back on Ethereum, can sign a claim, and have ETH ready for its gas. Sui approval comes almost at once, so most of the return trip’s remaining time is the claim step.

Related questions

How long does Suibridge take?

Ethereum to Sui waits about 13 minutes for Ethereum finality before the Sui mint completes automatically. Sui transactions finalize in under a second, so the Sui-to-Ethereum burn and approval can be quick; the claim still needs an Ethereum transaction, and its timing depends on that step.

Does Suibridge charge a bridge fee?

The route details here establish the gas costs, but they do not specify whether a separate bridge fee applies or its amount. Treat Ethereum and Sui gas as distinct from any bridge fee, and review the current terms for the route before you submit.

What should I know before claiming?

What happens if I never claim on Ethereum?

The return transfer remains unclaimed on Ethereum until you submit the claim transaction. The Sui asset has already been burned, but the Ethereum release requires your signed transaction and ETH for gas.

Can I bridge to a different Ethereum address than the sender?

The route facts here do not establish whether the claim can go to a different Ethereum address. Confirm the destination-address rules for the route before burning the asset on Sui.

Do Suibridge rate limits differ by token?

Rate limits apply per route and direction; the available details do not say whether they also vary by token. Check the specific asset and direction you plan to use, since a hold on one route need not stop the other.
