Why Mantle Bridge Transfers Stay Pending

A pending Mantle Bridge transfer may be awaiting Ethereum confirmation, proof or a separate claim; the direction tells you which step comes next.

Crypto Record EditorialSep 29, 20262 min read

A Mantle Bridge transfer stays pending while its message moves through the steps required on its source and destination networks. A deposit starts on Ethereum and completes when the corresponding balance is processed on Mantle; a withdrawal starts on Mantle and needs settlement on Ethereum before you can claim the funds.

What does pending mean on Mantle Bridge?

A bridge transfer is a message between two networks, not one transaction that instantly moves a balance. For a deposit, you sign a transaction on Ethereum. If you are sending an ERC-20 token, you may first need to approve the bridge contract to spend it; that approval only grants permission and does not deposit the token. After the deposit transaction is confirmed, its message must be processed on Mantle before the destination balance appears.

For that step, use the official Mantle Bridge app, which is the bridge for moving ETH, MNT and tokens between Ethereum and Mantle Network. It lets you begin the transfer between those networks; the deposit still needs confirmation on Ethereum and processing on Mantle before the destination balance is ready.

A withdrawal follows a different path. The Mantle transaction records the request, but it does not immediately add funds to your Ethereum balance. Mantle uses ZK validity proofs: a proof of network state must be verified on Ethereum before the withdrawal can be claimed. The current model does not require the old optimistic challenge period, but proof and settlement still take time.

Why is a Mantle withdrawal still pending?

Because the withdrawal has separate initiation and claim steps. Once its state is covered by a proof verified on Ethereum, the exit becomes claimable. You then send a claim transaction on Ethereum, which releases the funds to your wallet. That claim is a new transaction and needs ETH for gas, even when the asset being withdrawn is ETH.

Think of the withdrawal as a parcel moving through a depot: starting the request creates the parcel, settlement clears it, and the claim collects it. The analogy stops there; on-chain, the proof and claim are distinct protocol steps. A pending status can therefore mean the request is waiting for settlement, or that settlement is complete but the claim has not been sent.

What should you check when a transfer is pending?

Use the transaction hash to identify the last completed step, then check the address and network where the funds should arrive. The direction of travel determines what to look for:

  • Ethereum to Mantle: Confirm the deposit transaction, not just a token approval. Then check the destination address on Mantle for the processed balance.
  • Mantle to Ethereum: Check whether the withdrawal is awaiting proof and settlement or is ready for its separate Ethereum claim.
  • At the claim step: Make sure the same wallet has ETH on Ethereum to pay claim gas. Funds still held by the bridge cannot pay that fee.

A confirmed bridge transaction cannot be cancelled by closing the page. If the destination balance is not visible, first verify the receiving address and network, then check whether the destination message or withdrawal claim has completed. The practical rule is simple: deposits wait for destination processing; withdrawals wait for settlement and then a claim. Find the unfinished step before sending another transfer.
