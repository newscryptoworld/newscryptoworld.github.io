Mastercard opens AI agent payment APIs after six pilots

Mastercard opened Agent Pay for Machines to developers after pilots, using signed x402 payment vouchers to let AI agents buy services within approved budgets.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 21, 2026 · 01:40 UTC3 min read

Mastercard opened early access to its Agent Pay for Machines APIs on October 20, letting developers build and test payments by AI agents working within business-approved budgets. In its announcement, Mastercard said the system builds on x402, an open protocol for requesting payment online, and follows pilots with Cloudflare, Ant International, AIsa, Base, BVNK, Coinbase and Hype.

How does an AI agent pay for a service?

A business first gives the agent a task, permissions and a spending limit. When the agent reaches a paid service, the provider returns an HTTP “402 Payment Required” response. The agent then creates a digitally signed payment voucher: a promise to pay within its approved limit. The provider checks the voucher and delivers the service.

The voucher lets the agent pay as it works without setting up a separate account, API key or billing relationship with every provider. Mastercard says these interactions happen off-chain at machine speed, and providers can combine vouchers and redeem them later. It is like giving a worker an approved expense limit, then checking each purchase against it before handing over the goods.

What did the pilot show on Base?

At Cloudflare Connect, marketing agency Hype gave an agent a sample task: plan a campaign for a protein bar launch. The agent bought social media research from AIsa and eight campaign images through Cloudflare AI Gateway. Mastercard said the demo involved nine payments across the two merchants, all within Hype’s approved budget.

The Block’s report describes the crypto and settlement roles: Coinbase Developer Platform supplied wallet infrastructure to fund the agent, while Base handled on-chain settlement. BVNK acted as the agent wallet and used Mastercard’s Global Money Platform to handle payment and settlement in fiat or stablecoins. Sellers can receive payment in their preferred currency, token or rail, according to Mastercard.

What can developers access now?

The early access program provides buyer- and seller-side APIs, a toolkit, a sandbox and developer documentation. Developers can use them to build and test agent-to-agent commerce experiences. Mastercard says the system is intended for digital services such as data, AI models, computing power and APIs.

The change puts spending authority at the center of the payment flow: a business defines the task and cap, the agent requests a service, and the provider verifies a signed voucher before supplying it. The pilot demonstrated that flow across two providers. Early access now gives developers tools to test it beyond that demonstration.
