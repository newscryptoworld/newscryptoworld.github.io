Fairshake backs 32 House candidates after crypto bill stalls

Fairshake named 32 House incumbents for 2026 support, including six $1 million allocations, as Senate gridlock leaves its market-structure bill stalled.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 11, 2026 · 04:22 UTC3 min read

Fairshake, a major crypto campaign group, named 32 House incumbents it will support in the November midterms after the Senate blocked an industry-backed market-structure bill in September. The group’s list links its election spending to lawmakers who backed the bill: all 32 voted for it, according to the Associated Press report.

How will Fairshake direct its House spending?

Fairshake plans to support 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats. It disclosed six allocations of $1 million each: Democrats Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California, and Republicans French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. The group has not said how much it will spend on the other 26 candidates, the Roll Call account of the slate reported.

The list works like a map of where Fairshake wants its election spending to go. First, it identifies candidates the group considers supportive of crypto policy. Then it directs money toward those races. Fairshake describes its aim as backing candidates who support crypto in both parties. Its spokesperson said the group and its affiliates had supported 29 Democrats and 28 Republicans in primaries and special elections this year.

The six disclosed $1 million amounts are spending commitments, not a total for all 32 races. Fairshake did not provide a full House budget. The group and its affiliated PACs reported about $120 million in cash on hand at the end of August, according to AP.

What bill did all 32 candidates support?

The CLARITY Act would establish a federal framework for digital-asset markets, including rules for market operations and oversight. The House passed it in July 2025. It then languished in the Senate for more than a year as lawmakers disagreed over ethics safeguards, including limits addressing public officials’ crypto ventures.

In September, the Senate blocked the bill on a procedural vote. Democrats argued that it did not do enough to address President Donald Trump’s crypto investments. The disagreement left the industry’s effort to establish a market-structure framework stalled, with Fairshake turning attention to the candidates who had supported the measure.

How does the House slate compare with Fairshake’s other spending?

The House plan is one part of a broader campaign. In September, Fairshake announced nearly $30 million in spending against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio. Brown is challenging Republican Sen. Jon Husted. Fairshake also supported Senate Democrats Ruben Gallego of Arizona and Elissa Slotkin of Michigan in 2024, spending about $10 million on each, AP reported.

Fairshake has not said whether it will spend more in Senate or House races before November. The disclosed House allocations show which six candidates are getting the clearest stated support; the remaining 26 placements reveal the group’s wider bipartisan slate, but not the size of its planned spending in each race.
