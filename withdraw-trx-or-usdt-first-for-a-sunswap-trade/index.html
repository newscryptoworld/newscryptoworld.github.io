Withdraw TRX or USDT First for a SunSwap Trade?

Withdraw TRX or USDT from an exchange in the right order: send a small TRX test first, keep a fee reserve, then move TRC-20 USDT to TronLink.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 25, 2026 · 07:21 UTC3 min read

Withdraw TRX to your empty TronLink address first, because TRX activates the address and pays the Energy and Bandwidth needed for later USDT transfers and swaps.

SunSwap swaps TRC-20 tokens through liquidity pools on TRON, and the wallet needs TRX to cover network costs along the way. For a Binance move, this order prepares your first SunSwap trade after Binance.

What should you withdraw to TronLink first?

Send a small TRX test withdrawal first, then move the rest of your TRX reserve before withdrawing USDT.

  1. Copy your receiving address from TronLink. Make sure you are using the TRON wallet address you intend to fund.
  2. Withdraw a small amount of TRX from the exchange to that address. Select the TRON network for the withdrawal.

    Network note: Use TRON for TRX. For USDT, choose TRON (TRC-20); the exchange may label it “TRC20.” A matching address alone does not make a different network compatible.

  3. Wait for the test transfer to arrive, then confirm the transaction and TRX balance on TRONSCAN. This verifies that the address and network choice worked before you send more.
  4. Withdraw the remaining TRX you want to keep for fees to the same address. Leave a reserve beyond any TRX you plan to trade; network costs vary with the resources available to the wallet and the transactions it makes.
  5. Withdraw your USDT using the TRON (TRC-20) network and the same TronLink address.

    Token note: TRON’s USDT contract is TR7NHqjeKQxGTCi8q8ZY4pL8otSzgjLj6t. Match that contract on TRONSCAN before treating a displayed USDT token as the real asset; a similar name or symbol can belong to a lookalike token.

  6. Check TRONSCAN for both the TRX balance and the USDT token at your address. You are ready when the page shows the TRX reserve and USDT associated with the contract above.

TRON transactions use Bandwidth and Energy, or burn TRX when the wallet needs to cover a resource cost. USDT is a TRC-20 token, so sending it requires a contract transaction; swapping it on SunSwap also uses Energy. A wallet holding only USDT has no TRX to cover those costs, so the USDT transfer or swap can stall until TRX arrives.

The small test withdrawal is useful for two reasons: it activates a fresh address and checks that the exchange sent funds over TRON. Think of it as testing the route with a small parcel before sending the rest. Once TRONSCAN shows the test TRX, you have evidence that the chosen address and network worked. You can then send the reserve and USDT in sequence instead of discovering a network mismatch after a larger withdrawal.

Keep enough TRX for the USDT transfer and the trades you expect to make, with some left over. The amount needed depends on network resources and the transactions, so there is no single fee reserve that fits every wallet. Do not send all available TRX to a trade and leave the wallet unable to cover its next transaction.

Change one detail: suppose the TronLink address already has TRX from an earlier transfer. You can skip the small test deposit for address activation, but still confirm the address and TRON network before withdrawing USDT. The core answer holds: keep TRX available for fees before moving or swapping USDT.

Related questions

How much TRX to keep for SunSwap fees?

Keep a TRX reserve beyond the amount you intend to trade. The fee need varies with the Energy and Bandwidth available and the transactions your wallet makes.

Which network to pick when withdrawing USDT?

Choose TRON (TRC-20) on the exchange and confirm the received token’s contract on TRONSCAN.
