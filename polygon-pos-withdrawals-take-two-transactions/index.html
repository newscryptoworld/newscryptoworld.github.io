Polygon PoS withdrawals take two transactions

A Polygon PoS withdrawal burns the token first, then releases the Ethereum asset after a validator checkpoint; plan for two transactions and a wait.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 4, 20264 min read

Withdrawing from Polygon PoS takes two transactions: one burns the token on Polygon, and a later one releases the corresponding asset on Ethereum after a checkpoint. Between them, Polygon validators attest to the Polygon block containing the burn, and that checkpoint is submitted to Ethereum. The wait is part of the bridge process, not a third transaction you need to sign.

The Polygon Bridge’s token movement across chains follows a simple pattern: the asset on one side is removed before its counterpart becomes available on the other. The contracts and proof that enforce this pattern explain why the withdrawal has two distinct stages.

What happens when you start a Polygon PoS withdrawal?

The first transaction burns the bridged token on Polygon PoS. In the usual PoS Bridge flow, the Polygon token is the counterpart of an asset held on Ethereum. Burning it records that it should no longer circulate on Polygon, and gives the bridge a transaction to verify before it releases the Ethereum-side asset.

When you begin, check the asset, amount, destination network and wallet address shown by the bridge. The wallet must be able to sign on Polygon for the burn, then on Ethereum for the exit. The burn is not a reversible transfer back into your Polygon wallet: once submitted, the next step is to complete the withdrawal on Ethereum.

Why does the withdrawal wait for a checkpoint?

A checkpoint is a validator-submitted record that commits to a range of Polygon blocks on Ethereum. The withdrawal cannot be completed until that record covers the block containing your burn. The bridge uses the checkpoint to construct a proof that the burn happened on Polygon and is eligible for an exit on Ethereum.

Think of the checkpoint as a signed receipt that the Ethereum-side contract can check. Validators produce and submit these records as part of Polygon PoS operations; the bridge then uses one to verify your particular transaction. Checkpoint timing can vary, so a pending withdrawal may need more time before the exit transaction is available. A delay at this stage does not mean you should submit another burn.

How do you finish the withdrawal on Ethereum?

After the burn is checkpointed, return to the bridge interface and select the option to complete or exit the withdrawal. The interface prepares the proof, and the Ethereum-side bridge contract checks it against the checkpoint. If the proof is valid, the contract releases the corresponding asset on Ethereum to the destination address. This second transaction requires Ethereum gas.

Before signing, confirm that your wallet is connected to Ethereum and that the receiving address is the one you intend to use. You need enough ETH in that wallet to pay the network fee. The first transaction’s Polygon fee does not pay for this Ethereum transaction; each network charges for its own transaction.

What should you plan for before withdrawing?

Plan for two wallet confirmations and a wait between them. Your withdrawal is not complete when the Polygon burn succeeds: it is complete when the Ethereum exit transaction succeeds. Keep the burn transaction details so you can check its status and return to the pending withdrawal if you close the bridge page.

  • Check that the bridge supports the asset you want to withdraw.
  • Keep access to the same wallet and its signing method for both networks.
  • Budget for a Polygon fee at the burn stage and an Ethereum fee at the exit stage.
  • Wait for the checkpoint before trying to submit the Ethereum exit.

For most users, the practical choice is to use the standard PoS withdrawal when returning the bridged asset itself to Ethereum. It trades a checkpoint wait and two transactions for a bridge-enforced release tied to the Polygon burn. Check the status between stages, then sign the Ethereum exit once it is ready.
