Single-sided staking vs LP token staking: choose your risk layers

Single-sided staking vs LP token staking usually favors the single token on risk: LP staking adds impermanent loss and pool-contract risk to staking exposure.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 28, 2026 · 02:18 UTC4 min read

LP token staking usually carries more risk than single-sided staking because it adds impermanent loss and pool-contract risk to the staking contract’s risks.

  • Single-sided staking exposes you to one token’s price, contract risk and any slashing.
  • LP token staking adds a second asset, impermanent loss and the pool contract’s risk; a slash reduces your claim on both assets.

In Aave’s legacy Safety Module, users could stake stkAAVE or a Balancer AAVE LP token called stkABPT. Both could be slashed, but only stkABPT also carried impermanent loss. The Aave staking overview can help explain where staking fits into Aave; the comparison here is about risk layers.

Which risks change when you stake one token or an LP token?

Single-sided staking adds staking risks to one token’s price exposure; LP token staking adds the risks of holding a rebalanced pair. The differences turn on four things: price exposure, impermanent loss, contracts and slashing.

Price exposure

With single-sided staking, your position’s market value moves with the token you deposit. If you stake AAVE, for instance, you still bear AAVE’s price movements while the stake is subject to the staking arrangement’s rules.

An LP token represents a share of a pool holding two assets. Its value depends on both assets and on how much of each the pool holds. That means you take on price exposure to a pair, even if you began with one token and received the LP token in return.

Impermanent loss

Impermanent loss is the difference between the value of your pool position and what the same assets would be worth if you had simply held them. In a constant-product pool, trades change the pool’s balance: as one asset rises in relative price, traders buy it from the pool, leaving the pool with more of the asset that has become cheaper.

That rebalancing creates the loss relative to holding, and the gap can grow as the assets’ prices diverge. The Uniswap v2 Core whitepaper describes the constant-product mechanism behind this rebalancing. A single-sided stake has no pool rebalancing, so it does not add this particular risk.

Correlated assets can make the risk easy to overlook. When two assets track each other, their relative price may stay close, keeping impermanent loss near zero. If one depegs, the pool keeps trading against the imbalance: traders take the stronger asset and leave more of the weaker one behind. The pool can end up concentrated in the depegged asset, so the loss that seemed negligible can arrive all at once. Correlation reduces this risk while the peg holds; it does not remove it.

Contracts involved

A single-sided staker relies on the staking contract and the systems around the deposited token. An LP staker relies on those staking mechanisms and the pool contract that holds and rebalances the pair. Each contract adds another place where a flaw or failure could affect the position. A familiar token pair does not remove the pool contract from the chain of dependencies.

Slashing

Slashing reduces a staked position under the protocol’s rules, so check whether it applies and what stake it can reach. In Aave’s legacy Safety Module, both stkAAVE and stkABPT were slashable. A slash to stkABPT reduces your claim on the LP position, which represents both pool assets; it does not remove the pool’s separate impermanent-loss risk. Single-sided staking has no second pool asset to expose to that rebalancing loss.

Which option suits your position?

Single-sided staking suits you when you already hold the token and want fewer risk layers. You still accept that token’s price movements, the staking contract’s risks and any applicable slashing, but you avoid impermanent loss and a pool contract.

LP token staking may suit you when you want exposure to a pair and understand how its pool rebalances, including what a depeg could do to the holdings. Before choosing, compare the token price exposure, impermanent loss, contracts involved and slashing rules. If holding the token is already your plan and fewer risk layers matter more than pool exposure, single-sided staking is the simpler risk choice.
