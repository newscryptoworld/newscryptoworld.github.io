Crypto staking rewards: receipt versus sale

When are crypto staking rewards taxable? Often when you can access or control them; selling or swapping later may create a separate gain or loss, depending on local rules.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 24, 2026 · 08:04 UTC4 min read

After a staking reward becomes available to you, it may be taxable before you sell it; the tax point depends on your country and whether you can access or control the tokens. The reward and a later sale or swap are separate events, so keep a record of each.

When does receiving a staking reward count for tax?

Crypto staking rewards may count as income when you receive them, even if you keep the tokens. In the US, IRS guidance says staking rewards are included in income when you gain dominion and control over them, with their fair market value measured at that time. In the UK, HMRC says staking tokens received by an individual who is not trading are generally other taxable income, valued in pounds at receipt.

Access matters because a reward you cannot yet use may be treated differently from one you can transfer, sell or swap. Think of the receipt date as the starting point on a transaction timeline: first the reward becomes available, then you decide whether to hold or dispose of it. The precise tax point and category depend on your jurisdiction and the staking arrangement.

On shiba swap, a user may stake tokens through Bury and receive BONE rewards. A look at how shiba swap rewards and swaps are taxed can help put those steps in context. For tax records, note when each reward became accessible and its value in your local currency on that date.

How do access restrictions change the timing?

Compare two otherwise identical Bury cases: the same user stakes the same amount and earns the same BONE reward, but the reward is accessible on different dates. In the first case, the user can transfer or swap the BONE as soon as it appears. In the second, a contract restriction prevents the user from moving or using it until later.

The difference is control. Under the IRS approach, the first user may reach the income tax point when the reward becomes controllable; the second may reach it only when the restriction ends and control begins. The example illustrates why a transaction record or dashboard display alone may not settle the tax date. Check the rules that apply to your country and arrangement.

What happens when you sell or swap the reward?

A later disposal is a separate tax event from receiving the reward. In the UK, HMRC says an individual who keeps staking tokens may have to pay Capital Gains Tax when disposing of them. In the US, a later sale or swap may also require a separate gain or loss calculation under the applicable rules.

Say your accessible BONE reward is worth £20 when received, and you later swap it when its value is £28. The £20 is the receipt value to record for the income calculation; the later disposal is assessed separately, using the relevant tax rules and records to work out any gain or loss. The two figures relate to different events, so selling does not erase the original reward entry.

For each reward, record its date, amount and local-currency value when accessible. For each later sale or swap, record the date, amount disposed of and proceeds. Keep transaction confirmations and wallet records where available; the receipt value can help establish the starting value for calculating a later gain or loss. Tax categories, allowances and reporting requirements vary, so check the relevant tax authority’s rules or ask a qualified adviser.

Related questions

Are staking rewards taxable before you sell them?

Often, yes. US and UK guidance can tax accessible rewards at receipt, subject to local rules and the details of the arrangement.

Do you pay tax again when you sell staking rewards?

A later sale or swap may create a separate gain or loss, measured from the reward’s value when received under the rules that apply to you.

  • Record the reward when it becomes accessible, including its date, amount and local-currency value.
  • Track any later sale or swap as a separate event, with its date and proceeds.
  • Check the tax rules for your country and staking arrangement.
