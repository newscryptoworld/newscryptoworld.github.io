US secures authority over $1.2bn from Binance theft

The Justice Department secured court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in Binance theft funds as tracing and freezes continue across crypto services.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 22, 2026 · 09:58 UTC3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department has obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while tracing and freezes continue across crypto services. The department said the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and attributed it to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor, according to the Justice Department’s announcement.

How did authorities stop some of the stolen crypto?

After the theft, U.S. government agencies worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to trace and freeze funds. A VASP is a company that provides cryptocurrency services. The Justice Department says those joint efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets. The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of that amount; it is not an additional sum on top of the frozen funds.

The process has several steps. Investigators trace the stolen assets as they move between crypto addresses. Law enforcement and service providers can then act on identified addresses or funds to block transfers. The FBI has urged VASPs to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses TraderTraitor is using. The department says the hackers have continued laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, so efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the funds remain ongoing.

Who is affected by the seizure authority?

Binance is the exchange from which the assets were stolen. The FBI has previously attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, and the Justice Department described the stolen amount as more than $6 billion. The court action gives the government legal authority to seize or restrain a portion of the funds that have already been frozen. It does not mean that all stolen cryptocurrency has been recovered: the department says more than $2.7 billion has been frozen so far.

For holders and companies that handle crypto, the action shows how cooperation between investigators and service providers can interrupt transfers after a theft. The Justice Department says the agencies are still tracing the assets as they pass through services used to move or obscure cryptocurrency.

What happens to the funds now?

Seizure authority is a legal step toward taking control of assets; it does not by itself decide who ultimately receives them. In a typical case, the government files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to the recovered funds, the Associated Press reported.

The department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance funds are continuing. The next question is how much of the frozen crypto can be secured through the legal process and returned to a legitimate claimant.
