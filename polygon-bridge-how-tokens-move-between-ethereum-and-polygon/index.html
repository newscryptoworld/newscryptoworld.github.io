Polygon Bridge: How Tokens Move Between Ethereum and Polygon

Polygon Bridge moves supported tokens between Ethereum and Polygon PoS by locking and minting on deposits, then burning and claiming on withdrawals.

Crypto Record EditorialSep 28, 20263 min read

Polygon Bridge moves supported tokens between Ethereum and Polygon PoS by locking them on one chain and releasing or creating their counterpart on the other. To move tokens in, you choose the source and destination networks, approve the token if asked, and confirm a deposit transaction in your wallet. The bridge contract locks the tokens on Ethereum; a corresponding amount is then minted on Polygon PoS. Think of it as checking a coat: the original stays in one place while a matching claim is issued elsewhere.

Polygon’s own terminology helps when comparing the steps: the Polygon Bridge moves assets between networks, while your wallet still signs each transaction. Supported tokens and available routes depend on the networks selected. Check the asset and destination network before you begin.

How do I use Polygon Bridge to deposit tokens?

For an Ethereum-to-Polygon PoS transfer, select Ethereum as the source and Polygon PoS as the destination, connect your wallet, then choose a supported token and amount. The bridge interface shows the route and estimated fees before you submit. Keep ETH in your wallet for Ethereum transaction fees, including any separate token approval the contract needs before it can move your funds.

After approval, confirm the deposit transaction. The token contract grants the bridge permission to transfer the specified amount; the bridge then locks that amount on Ethereum. Once the deposit is processed, the matching bridged asset appears at the destination address on Polygon PoS. The balance may take time to update, so check the transaction status before attempting another transfer.

How do I withdraw tokens back to Ethereum?

A Polygon PoS withdrawal runs in the other direction, but it has an extra step. The bridge burns the bridged tokens on Polygon, then Ethereum releases the corresponding locked tokens after the withdrawal is confirmed and the required checkpoint and proof are available. In practice, you start the withdrawal on Polygon, wait for the bridge interface to show that you can claim, then switch to Ethereum and sign the claim transaction.

Keep POL on Polygon PoS to pay for the withdrawal transaction and ETH on Ethereum to pay for the final claim. The claim is not optional: starting a withdrawal does not by itself put tokens back in your Ethereum wallet. Follow the status shown for that transfer and claim when the interface makes it available. Ethereum’s fees and the wait for the withdrawal process are the main costs in time and gas on this route.

What should I check before bridging tokens?

Confirm the selected networks, token, amount, and receiving address before signing. A token’s name can appear on more than one network, but that does not mean every version is the same asset or supported by the route. The bridge’s supported-token list determines whether a transfer can complete.

  • Keep the source network’s native gas token for the deposit or withdrawal transaction: ETH on Ethereum and POL on Polygon PoS.
  • For withdrawals, keep enough ETH at the receiving wallet to pay for the claim.
  • Read the wallet prompt and check the transaction details before signing. A bridge transfer can be difficult or impossible to reverse after submission.
  • Use the transfer status to distinguish a pending deposit from a withdrawal that is waiting for its claim step.

For most users, the sensible approach is to bridge only the amount needed for the next task, then confirm it arrived before moving more. A deposit creates a token balance on Polygon PoS; a withdrawal burns that balance and requires a separate Ethereum claim. Knowing which network holds the funds at each step makes the process easier to track.
