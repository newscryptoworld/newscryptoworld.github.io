How to stay safe using Matcha swap with your wallet

How to stay safe using Matcha swap: use a saved link, protect your seed phrase, limit token approvals, verify addresses and know how to respond to a bad signature.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 24, 2026 · 03:22 UTC4 min read

Reach Matcha swap through a saved link, protect your seed phrase, and read each wallet prompt before signing. The interface is non-custodial and built by 0x Protocol, so your wallet authorizes the permissions and transactions. This Matcha swap link takes you to the topic for more on using the token exchange while protecting your wallet.

How do fake sites and tokens put a swap at risk?

Phishing sites imitate a swap interface to steal wallet access or trick you into signing a harmful request. Search ads can lead to copies that ask for a seed phrase or present a drainer signature as a routine connection or trade. A legitimate service never needs your seed phrase to connect your wallet; Ethereum.org’s security guidance explains how to spot phishing and protect recovery phrases.

Bookmark the real site after you verify its address, then use that saved link instead of an ad or message. Keep a separate swap wallet with only the funds you intend to trade. That limits the assets at risk if you later approve the wrong spender or sign a malicious transaction.

A token ticker is not proof of identity. Copycat tokens can use the same name and symbol as a real project. Before a Matcha DEX swap, compare the token’s contract address with the address published by the project through its official source. A familiar ticker paired with a different contract is a different token.

What should you check before signing?

Read the wallet prompt as a permission request, not as a formality. A token approval lets a spender move a particular token from your wallet, up to the allowance you authorize. Approvals remain active until you revoke them, even after the swap is complete.

When the wallet offers an editable spending cap, enter the amount you plan to swap instead of accepting an unlimited default. The cap limits how much that spender can take under that approval. If the spender is later exploited, a limited approval leaves less exposure than an unlimited allowance. MetaMask explains how to set a custom token approval spending cap.

Before signing, confirm the token, amount, spender and requested action in the wallet. Reject prompts you do not understand, especially unexpected approvals or signatures that appear after you have stopped a trade. A wallet connection by itself is different from an approval: the approval is the on-chain permission to move tokens.

Walk through a small swap this way: open the bookmarked site, leaving the browser at the verified address; connect the separate wallet, which shares its public address but not its seed phrase; select the token by contract address, leaving the intended asset chosen; set the approval cap to the trade amount, leaving only that allowance authorized; review and sign the swap, leaving the transaction submitted. At each step, the wallet prompt is the point to pause and confirm.

What do you do after a bad approval or signature?

Revoke a bad token approval promptly using the Etherscan Token Approval Checker for Ethereum, then confirm the approval is gone. Revocation requires another on-chain transaction. MetaMask’s allowance revocation instructions explain the process and distinguish revoking an approval from disconnecting a site.

If you entered your seed phrase on a suspicious site, treat the wallet as compromised: create a new wallet and move remaining funds there. Revoking approvals does not make an exposed seed phrase safe. If you signed a transaction that transferred tokens, Ethereum transactions cannot generally be reversed; save the transaction details and do not trust anyone offering recovery in exchange for your seed phrase or an upfront payment.

The detail that shows your swap is limited is the approval amount in the wallet: it matches the trade amount, so the spender’s permission is bounded.

Questions swappers ask next

How do I check which approvals my wallet has given?

Use the Etherscan Token Approval Checker for the relevant Ethereum address to review token allowances and identify approvals you no longer want. Verify you are using the official Etherscan domain before connecting or signing anything.

Is a hardware wallet worth it for occasional swaps?

It can help protect keys by keeping them offline, but it does not identify fake tokens or make a risky signature safe. Read the transaction details on the device before approving.

Can a stolen swap be reversed?

Usually, no. A confirmed on-chain transfer is generally irreversible. Act quickly to revoke remaining bad approvals, move funds if the seed phrase was exposed, and ignore paid recovery offers.
