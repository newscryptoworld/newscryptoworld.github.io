Reconcile Every Treasury Bridge Transfer

A clean bridge close matches the source debit to the destination credit, accounts for fees and timing, and leaves an auditable record for every transfer.

Crypto Record EditorialSep 29, 20263 min read

Treasury bridge reconciliation closes a transfer by matching value leaving one chain with value arriving on another, then recording fees, status, and any timing gap. The process starts with an instruction: which asset should move, from which treasury address, on which network, and to which destination. The treasury then follows that instruction through the bridge contract and checks the resulting records on both chains.

What records do you need to reconcile a bridge transfer?

You need the treasury’s transfer instruction, the source-chain transaction, and the destination-chain transaction or event. Record the asset and network on each side, sender and recipient addresses, amounts, transaction hashes, and the bridge’s transfer or message identifier when available. A bridge may lock an asset on the source chain and mint a representation on the destination, or burn on one side and release on the other. Match the events that belong to the same transfer; a shared ticker alone does not prove that two tokens represent the same asset. For route-specific detail, the fuller guide to manta bridge explains why asset, network, and return plan belong in the same check.

Think of the transfer as a two-sided journal entry: the source record shows what left, and the destination record shows what arrived. Unlike a simple internal ledger move, the two records may appear at different times because each chain confirms transactions on its own schedule.

How do you match the amounts on both chains?

Start with the instructed amount, then account separately for bridge fees, network gas, and any amount retained or deducted by the route. A source debit may be larger than the destination credit because of fees. Gas paid to a validator or network is also a cost, but it is not automatically part of the bridge’s transfer fee. Record each cost in the appropriate account instead of forcing the two token amounts to match.

Check token decimals and the asset representation before comparing amounts. A destination token can represent the same underlying asset while having a different contract address. Use the bridge’s message or event records to connect the representations, then compare amounts in a common unit. Mark a transfer complete only after the destination-side event is present and meets the treasury’s confirmation policy.

  • Match the source instruction to the source transaction and transfer identifier.
  • Match that identifier to the destination event and recipient address.
  • Reconcile the source debit to destination credit, with fees and gas itemized.
  • Keep the hashes, timestamps, asset identifiers, and status in the close record.

What should you do when a bridge transfer does not match?

Leave the transfer open as an exception until its state is understood. A source transaction can succeed while the destination message remains pending; a failed route may instead show a refund or return event. Check the bridge’s recorded status and both chains’ events before retrying, because a delayed message and a failed transfer require different actions. If the destination amount or recipient differs from the instruction, preserve the evidence and route it through the treasury’s approval process rather than posting a balancing adjustment.

Close each transfer with a clear status: completed, pending, refunded, or under review. That makes the ledger explain both where the value moved and why any gap remains. The practical rule is simple: match the event trail first, book the costs separately, and close only when the destination outcome is supported by records.
