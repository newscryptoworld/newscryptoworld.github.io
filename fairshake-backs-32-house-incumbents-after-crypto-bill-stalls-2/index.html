Fairshake backs 32 House incumbents after crypto bill stalls

Fairshake named 32 House incumbents for midterm support and committed $6 million to six, as the Senate’s stalled Clarity Act leaves crypto rules unresolved.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 9, 2026 · 19:55 UTC3 min read

Crypto political group Fairshake says it will support 32 House incumbents in November’s midterm elections, naming 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats after the Senate stalled the industry’s priority market structure bill, according to The Associated Press’s report on the slate. Fairshake committed $1 million in spending apiece to six candidates. It did not say how much it plans to spend on the other 26.

How will Fairshake’s support work?

Fairshake named candidates it plans to support, but disclosed specific spending only for six. The three Republicans are House Financial Services Committee members French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. The three Democrats are Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California. Roll Call’s account of the announcement reports the six $1 million commitments and identifies the candidates.

Fairshake’s list includes incumbents from both parties. The group says all 32 supported the Clarity Act, which passed the House in July 2025. Its spokesperson, Geoff Vetter, described Fairshake as issue-focused and said it backs pro-crypto candidates in both parties. Fairshake and its affiliated PACs reported about $120 million in cash at the end of August, AP reported. That figure is the groups’ reported cash on hand, not a disclosed budget for these 32 House races.

What would the stalled Clarity Act have changed?

The bill would establish a federal regulatory framework for digital assets and divide oversight between federal regulators, according to AP. For crypto firms, the proposed framework was meant to provide more certainty about which rules applied and who would oversee the market.

The House passed the measure, but the Senate did not advance it. The bill stalled amid disagreements over ethics guardrails, including concerns about public officials profiting from crypto ventures. In September, the Senate blocked it on a procedural vote. That vote stopped the bill from moving forward; it was not a final vote on whether to enact the measure.

Why is the House slate tied to the bill’s setback?

Fairshake’s selections connect campaign support to the legislation’s next step: lawmakers who return to the House could consider crypto rules again in a future Congress. The group’s slate is therefore a way to support incumbents who backed the bill, while the Senate’s failure to advance it leaves the proposed federal framework unresolved.

The immediate spending picture remains partial. Fairshake named all 32 House candidates but specified $1 million apiece for only six. It also did not say whether it plans to spend more in House or Senate races before November, AP reported. The announced slate signals whom the group wants to support; the total it will spend across those races is still unknown.
