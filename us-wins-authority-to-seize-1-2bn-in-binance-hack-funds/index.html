US wins authority to seize $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

US courts authorized seizure or restraint of over $1.2 billion in funds stolen from Binance, while investigators and crypto firms keep tracing and freezing assets.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 18, 2026 · 08:14 UTC3 min read

The US Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The FBI attributes the theft, which took place on or about Oct. 29, to North Korean military actors it calls TraderTraitor. In its Nov. 12 announcement, the department said government agencies and private-sector partners had frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets so far.

How did investigators stop the funds?

Crypto moves between wallet addresses through transactions recorded on blockchains. Investigators can follow those transactions as funds move, including when actors send them through bridges, mixers or exchanges. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor continues to launder assets through those services, and that tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts remain underway.

Authorities also work with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs. These firms can help interdict assets they identify as stolen. The FBI has urged providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the Binance funds, including transactions derived from those addresses. The process resembles flagging a parcel as it moves through a delivery network: the record of each transfer can help locate it, while cooperation at the service handling it can stop it moving further.

Who is affected by the freeze and seizure?

The theft hit Binance, the cryptocurrency exchange, and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets, according to the Justice Department. The FBI named the suspected actors TraderTraitor and attributed them to North Korea. The government’s action concerns assets identified as stolen; the announcement does not say that Binance customers have received funds back.

The $2.7 billion figure covers funds frozen through combined government and private-sector efforts. The separately reported $1.2 billion is the portion for which the department says it obtained court authorization to seize or restrain. Those figures describe different stages of the response, and the authorized amount is part of the larger frozen total.

What does court authorization change?

A freeze can prevent assets from moving while investigators work. Court authorization gives the government legal authority to seize or restrain the specified funds. It does not, by itself, establish who will ultimately receive them. The Associated Press reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The next steps depend on tracing the assets, acting on court authority and resolving any forfeiture claims. The Justice Department says those efforts are continuing as the suspected actors move funds through crypto services. The announcement describes a substantial disruption to the theft’s proceeds, but leaves the final disposition of the assets unresolved.
