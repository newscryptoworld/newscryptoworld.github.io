Injective’s Veyra upgrade clears vote, targets Nov. 24 activation

Injective’s Veyra upgrade passed governance with 42.69% turnout and targets a halt at block 193,145,000 on Nov. 24 before the chain resumes on v1.21.0.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 24, 2026 · 04:42 UTC3 min read

Injective’s Veyra mainnet upgrade passed governance and is targeted to activate on Nov. 24, when the chain reaches block 193,145,000, according to the Injective Proposal 717 governance record. The plan is to halt block production at that height and resume with the v1.21.0 version of Injective’s chain software.

The proposal was submitted on Nov. 12 and voting closed Nov. 16. The governance record shows 42.69% turnout, above the 33.4% quorum requirement. Of the voting power recorded, 16,236,896 INJ voted yes, 80,589 voted no, and 7,745,866 abstained. Those are token-weighted votes: the figures measure voting power, not the number of people who supported the upgrade.

What changes in the Veyra software

Veyra adds Inter-Blockchain Communication, or IBC, capabilities for moving assets between Injective’s Ethereum Virtual Machine environment and connected chains. The EVM is the part of Injective that runs Ethereum-compatible contracts. IBC provides a route for those assets to move across networks; it does not itself issue new tokenized assets.

The proposal also adds request-for-quote infrastructure and expands atomic-swap functionality. An RFQ lets a trader ask for prices before choosing a trade. An atomic swap links the exchange of assets so that the swap either completes under its conditions or does not. Together, those tools are intended to support trading and settlement workflows alongside cross-chain asset movement.

The proposal describes Veyra as groundwork for CypherOS, Injective’s planned privacy platform. That platform is not part of this activation: The Defiant’s report on the approved proposal says CypherOS remains on the roadmap. The distinction matters because Veyra’s approved scope adds connectivity and trading infrastructure, while confidential trading is a later project.

Validators coordinate the chain restart

When Injective reaches the upgrade height, the chain is due to stop producing blocks while validators move to the new software. Think of it as a coordinated software changeover: the network pauses at an agreed point so validators can restart together on the same version. The date is a target tied to reaching block 193,145,000, not a specified time of day.

The Defiant reports that Injective’s upgrade guide instructs operators to wait for the halt before replacing the active binary, then verify the new software and restart their nodes. The governance vote authorizes this planned change; it does not establish that the activation has already happened. At the time of the proposal, the next milestone was the targeted Nov. 24 halt and restart. After that, the practical test will be whether the new cross-chain and trading functions operate on mainnet as intended.
