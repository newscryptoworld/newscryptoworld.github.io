Fairshake backs 32 House incumbents after crypto bill stalls

Fairshake will back 32 House incumbents, with $1 million each for six, after the Senate blocked the CLARITY Act, keeping crypto market rules in play for 2026 races.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 10, 2026 · 23:10 UTC3 min read

Fairshake, a major crypto campaign group, said Monday it will support 32 House incumbents in November’s midterms, including six with $1 million in spending each. The slate targets lawmakers who backed the industry’s market-structure bill, which stalled in the Senate, and puts election spending behind candidates who could revisit crypto rules in the next Congress, according to The Associated Press report on Fairshake’s slate.

How will Fairshake spread its support across the House races?

Fairshake named 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats. It disclosed $1 million in spending for each of three Democrats: Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California. Three Republicans—French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan—were also slated for $1 million each.

The group did not disclose the amounts for the other 26 candidates. Its spokesperson, Geoff Vetter, told AP that Fairshake backs candidates in both parties based on their support for crypto. The group and its affiliated PACs reported about $120 million in cash at the end of August, AP reported. That balance gives the network funds to deploy, but it does not establish how much it will spend across these 32 House races.

What did the candidates support, and where did the bill stop?

All 32 candidates supported the CLARITY Act, the crypto industry’s market-structure bill. The House passed it in July 2025. The proposal would create a federal regulatory framework for digital assets, with oversight divided between federal regulators, according to Roll Call’s report on Fairshake’s House spending.

The bill then languished in the Senate for more than a year. Lawmakers failed to agree on ethics safeguards addressing whether public officials could profit from crypto ventures, among other provisions. In September, the Senate blocked the measure on a procedural vote. The House slate links Fairshake’s election support to lawmakers who backed the bill, while the Senate vote leaves its proposed rules unpassed.

What does the slate tell voters about the group’s spending?

The six disclosed allocations total $6 million, but they are only part of the 32-candidate effort. Fairshake has not said how much it will spend on the remaining House races or whether it will put more money into House or Senate contests before November, AP reported.

The House plan follows a separate Fairshake announcement in September to spend nearly $30 million opposing Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio. That contrast shows the group’s support is not distributed evenly across races: it has named six specific House allocations and a much larger planned effort against one Senate candidate. The next Congress will decide whether the stalled CLARITY Act returns to negotiations.
