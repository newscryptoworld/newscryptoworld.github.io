Fill-or-kill vs partially fillable: choose by order size

Fill-or-kill vs partially fillable limit orders: choose fill-or-kill when you need the whole amount; choose partial fills when liquidity is thin.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 21, 2026 · 19:06 UTC4 min read

Choose partially fillable for a large order or a thin market; choose fill-or-kill when only the full amount is useful. The right setting depends on how much liquidity is available at your limit price and whether receiving less than your target amount would help.

A limit order sets the price you are willing to trade at. Its fill setting determines how much of the order can execute when liquidity becomes available. Fill-or-kill requires the whole amount to fill at once; a partially fillable order can execute in smaller portions.

How do fill-or-kill and partially fillable limit orders differ?

Fill-or-kill executes the full order in one settlement or leaves it unfilled. Partially fillable lets any available portion execute at your limit price or better, with the unfilled remainder staying open.

Think of a queue at a counter: fill-or-kill waits until enough is ready to serve the entire group; a partially fillable order takes what is ready and waits for the rest. In trading, that “ready” amount is available liquidity at a price that meets your limit.

Take a 50,000 USDC buy limit with 20,000 USDC of liquidity available at the limit price. A partially fillable order can buy 20,000 USDC worth now and leave the rest open. A fill-or-kill order buys nothing because the full amount is not available for that fill.

That difference matters when a large fill-or-kill order meets limited depth. The market price can briefly touch your limit, yet the order may still not fill: the touch only matters if enough liquidity is there to execute the entire amount in one settlement. With a partially fillable order, some liquidity at your price can execute the first portion, and later portions can fill in separate settlements. Those fills accumulate until the order completes or expires.

Partial fills improve the chance that some of the order executes, but they can leave you with only part of the position you planned. That may be useful when building a position gradually. It can be inconvenient when the full amount is needed for a specific trade or plan.

Which limit order setting should you choose?

Choose based on order size versus available liquidity, and on whether a partial position helps. Use fill-or-kill if only the complete amount is useful. Use partially fillable if any executed portion helps and you are comfortable waiting for the remainder.

  • Choose fill-or-kill when you need the full amount or want the order to execute as one complete fill.
  • Choose partially fillable when the order is large relative to available liquidity, the market is thin, or accumulating the position over several fills is acceptable.

For a small order well inside the market’s available depth, both settings usually fill identically: enough liquidity exists for the whole amount, so the partial-fill option has no extra work to do.

Do not confuse a limit price with a fill setting. The limit price says what price you will accept; the fill setting says whether the whole amount must execute at once. Treating a price touch as a promise of a full fill can lead you to leave fill-or-kill selected when the available depth is too small.

Related

Why is my limit order only partially filled?

A limit order can be partially filled when it allows partial execution and only some of the order’s size can trade at your price or better. The remaining amount may fill in later settlements if liquidity becomes available before the order expires.

Can I cancel the unfilled rest of a partial order?

Usually, you can request cancellation of the unfilled remainder while leaving amounts already filled as completed trades. Check the order’s status and the interface’s cancellation controls; a cancellation may not prevent a portion that is already being settled.

Would you still find the position useful if only part of the order filled? If yes, choose partially fillable; if you need the whole amount, choose fill-or-kill.
