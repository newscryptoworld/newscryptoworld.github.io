How to Read a Cross-Chain Swap Quote

A cross-chain swap quote combines token prices, bridge fees, execution limits and timing; read each field to compare what arrives and what can still change.

Crypto Record EditorialOct 15, 2026 · 21:18 UTC4 min read

A cross-chain swap quote estimates how much of a chosen token should arrive on another chain, after swaps, bridge costs and execution limits. To read it, trace the route from the token you send to the token you receive, then check the minimum output, fees and timing. A route may swap before the bridge, after it, or on both sides. Those steps affect the estimate and how much of it depends on market prices.

What steps does a cross-chain swap quote include?

A quote usually begins with your origin chain, destination chain, input token, output token and amount. A routing service uses those choices to assemble one or more transactions. It may find a market to exchange your input token before the transfer, a bridge to move value across chains, and another market to produce the requested output token at the destination.

Read the route in that order. An origin swap changes what enters the bridge; the bridge applies its own fee and transfer conditions; a destination swap changes what reaches your wallet. Some routes skip either swap because the same token can cross directly or because it is already the requested output. The route name alone does not tell you which steps are included.

Apps choose among routes with different costs, speeds and swap paths. For more on how Acrossbridge apps choose routes, see the explanation of that selection. For any quote, expand the route details and check which chain each swap uses. Think of the route like a trip with connections: the destination is the same, but each transfer point can change the fare and arrival time.

Which quote fields show what you may receive?

For an exact-input quote, the amount you send is fixed and the output is an estimate. A minimum-output field sets a lower bound the route is expected to enforce at execution, if the transaction and its conditions support that protection. For an exact-output quote, the requested receive amount is fixed and the input estimate can vary. Check which amount the quote treats as fixed before comparing offers.

Look for the following fields and how they relate:

  • Estimated output: the quoted amount of the destination token at current prices and route conditions.
  • Minimum output: the least output allowed by the quote’s execution conditions; it may be lower than the estimate.
  • Fees: separate bridge, swap, network or app charges where shown. A fee may be deducted from the input or output, so check the units and where it is charged.
  • Price impact and slippage: price impact reflects how the trade size affects a market price; slippage is the allowed change between quoting and execution.

Do not compare only the estimated output. Compare the minimum output and fees in the same token, and confirm whether the estimate already subtracts those costs. If the quote lists network gas separately, include it in the total cost you expect to pay. An app fee, when present, is another deduction, not a bridge fee.

How can you compare route time and execution risk?

Check whether the quote gives an expected fill time, an expiry, or both. The time is an estimate, not a promise: a route may depend on a relayer or other service completing a destination-chain transaction after the origin transaction is submitted. Expiry tells you when the quoted conditions may stop being valid. A quote that has expired should be refreshed before signing.

Then check what you must authorize. An allowance lets a contract spend a specific token amount; the swap transaction carries the instructions for the route. Confirm the input amount, destination token, recipient address and minimum output in the wallet prompt. A quote can change before submission as prices, liquidity or fees move, and a transaction can fail if its execution conditions are no longer met.

For most readers, the better route is the one whose minimum output and total cost suit the trade, with timing that fits the need. A faster estimate may come with a lower output or higher fee; a cheaper route may take longer. Read the quote as a sequence of conversions and transfers, not a single exchange rate. That makes it clearer what is fixed, what can move, and what will land in the destination wallet.
