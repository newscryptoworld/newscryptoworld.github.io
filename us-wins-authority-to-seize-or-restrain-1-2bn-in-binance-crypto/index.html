US wins authority to seize or restrain $1.2bn in Binance crypto

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of crypto stolen from Binance, part of a wider $2.7 billion freeze.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 20, 2026 · 12:21 UTC3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the department said Thursday. The assets are part of a wider pool of more than $2.7 billion frozen by U.S. agencies and private-sector partners after the October theft.

How did authorities trace and freeze the stolen crypto?

The department says members of the North Korean military, identified by the FBI as the group TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around Oct. 29. Investigators and law enforcement partners then worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict the assets. These providers can block transactions or freeze assets held through their services.

The Justice Department’s announcement says the stolen funds have moved through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges as investigators trace them. A bridge moves assets between blockchains; a mixer combines transactions to make their paths harder to follow. The FBI has urged VASPs to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the Binance funds.

Think of a freeze as putting a lock on assets while investigators follow the trail. It can stop them moving through a service, but it does not by itself settle who legally owns them.

What does the court authorization change?

The government says more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds has been frozen so far. Within that total, the Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. The wording matters: the announcement describes legal authority over assets already caught up in the wider effort, not a separate $1.2 billion recovery on top of the frozen amount.

Seizure or restraint is also not the same as returning funds to Binance or its customers. The Associated Press report says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Who is affected as the recovery effort continues?

Binance is the exchange from which the assets were reported stolen. The scale of the theft and the ongoing effort to trace and block the funds make the next steps relevant to the exchange and anyone with a claim to recovered assets. But the Justice Department says tracing, freezing, seizing and forfeiting the cryptocurrency are still ongoing, as TraderTraitor actors continue to move it through bridges, mixers and exchanges.

The court authorization gives the government a legal route to pursue a portion of the frozen assets. The eventual disposition of that crypto remains subject to the forfeiture process and further recovery work.
