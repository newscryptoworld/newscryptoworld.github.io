US wins court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of stolen Binance crypto, while tracing and recovery efforts continue.

Crypto Record EditorialNov 15, 2026 · 13:14 UTC3 min read

The US Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the department said on Nov. 12. The order covers part of the stolen assets already frozen through government and private-sector efforts. It does not mean the money has been returned to Binance. The department’s announcement of the court-authorized action says tracing and recovery work is still under way.

How did authorities move from tracing to a court order?

The theft happened on or about Oct. 29. The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. Investigators and law-enforcement partners then worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to trace the assets and freeze them. A VASP is a business that handles crypto transfers or custody, such as an exchange.

According to the department, those joint efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets. The court-authorized amount—more than $1.2 billion—is part of that frozen total. The Associated Press reported on the FBI and Justice Department announcement and noted that a forfeiture claim would typically go before a judge, who decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Who is involved in holding or recovering the crypto?

Binance is the exchange from which the assets were stolen. The Justice Department names its National Security Division, Criminal Division, the US Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia and law-enforcement partners as participants in the response. It says its teams coordinated with VASPs to interdict the assets. Those service providers can freeze assets they control or encounter, helping prevent further transfers while investigators pursue legal steps.

For readers, a freeze is like a bank account being locked: it can block movement, but it does not decide who owns the balance. Crypto transfers are recorded on blockchains, and the stolen assets can still be moved through services that connect networks or obscure transaction trails. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

What does the order change for the stolen funds?

The order gives the department court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the assets already frozen. “Seize or restrain” describes legal control; it does not establish that Binance or its customers have received the funds. The next steps include continuing to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets. If the government pursues forfeiture, a judge can determine who has a legitimate claim.

The FBI has also urged VASPs to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses it links to TraderTraitor’s laundering activity. The department says recovery efforts remain ongoing. The announcement therefore marks a legal step in the process, while the amount ultimately returned and its recipients remain unresolved.
